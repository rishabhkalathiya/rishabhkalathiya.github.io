Workato · Connector SDK

Cutting task usage ~70% around Airtable's 10-record limit

Problem
Hundreds of thousands of Salesforce records sync into Airtable every day. Airtable's bulk API takes 10 records per call, so the recipe looped batch after batch and burned tasks on every iteration. One recipe was consuming over 10% of the workspace's annual task quota. Moving to another system would have meant giving up Airtable's top-of-the-line features, so I built a workaround without compromising the business outcome.
Approach
A custom step built with the Workato Connector SDK. It accepts up to 500 records in a single batch, chunks them into groups of 10 and sends each group to Airtable through the bulk API. If a chunk fails, it falls back to individual records and retries the upsert one by one. Only the records that still fail are collected and written to an error log at the end of the run, instead of breaking the execution. I built it AI-assisted through Claude and Cursor, then tested it against real volume. The tooling got me to a working draft faster, and the design decisions were mine.
Result
About 70% fewer recipe tasks, measured from Workato task counts, and no more whole-batch failures. Before, one bad record could break the whole execution. Now it only costs that record. The lesson: a platform limit is a design constraint, not a reason to migrate or overpay. Engineer around it and keep the capabilities you chose the platform for.
Zendesk · Licensing

A ~$2M Zendesk license request cut by 40%, roughly $800k saved

Problem
A proposal to approve roughly $2M for a pool of Zendesk agent licenses over two years was moving through the Jira queue. I spotted it in tickets from another operations team. Many agents had been inactive for more than 60 days, and some still had over 2,000 tickets assigned to them.
Approach
I ran the discovery session with the Zendesk admin on the CS team, then proposed a human-in-the-loop Workato flow through Slackbot. Each inactive agent's profile and ticket count goes to their manager in Slack. The manager picks one of two options: take over the tickets and downgrade the agent, or do nothing. On a choice, the recipe reassigns the tickets, downgrades the license and confirms back to the manager.
Result
The license count came in 40% lower, saving roughly $800k over the two-year term. Long-stuck customer queries got attention again, which improved support, and Workato became the team's go-to tool for automation as well as integration.
Workato · Sync at volume

One two-way sync across Salesforce, Zendesk and NetSuite, 400k+ records a day

Problem
Sales, support and finance each worked from their own copy of the customer. Data moved in delayed batches or by hand, and when a record failed, nobody knew.
Approach
Salesforce starts the sync to Zendesk and NetSuite, and changes flow back to Salesforce, using event triggers and scheduled runs together. A timestamp check decides which change wins, so write-backs never loop or overwrite each other. The hard parts were API rate limits at volume, mismatched field mappings, and duplicates and ordering. Failed records are collected, grouped by error type, record type and source system, and sent to the sync owners every day.
Result
Data stays current in near real time, the manual loads are gone, failures reach the people who can fix them, and sales, support and finance work from the same numbers.
Workato · Optimization

Optimizing 10+ task-hungry recipes to cut platform task usage

Problem
Core recipes and integrations were eating the platform's task budget. Workato's usage reports showed which ones.
Approach
Bulk actions instead of one-record loops, filters placed earlier so unneeded runs never start, lookup tables and caching, and Workato SQL collections to remove loops and repeated validation steps. Same business outcome, fewer tasks.
Result
More than 60% fewer tasks on average across the major recipes, out of 10+ optimized.
